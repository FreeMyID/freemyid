# Marketing copy — Free My ID

Draft bank, 2026-11-06. Follows docs/POSITIONING.md (accurate, calm, patriotic not partisan, never mock). Before anything goes public, check every factual line against a cited source.

## Why not "cyber terrorists"

The user's idea was "Free your information from cyber terrorists". The energy is right, but the claim is wrong. The sites we target are legal businesses, so calling them terrorists is false. It invites a defamation letter from a company with lawyers, and it reads like the scam ads this audience already distrusts. What's documented is that **strangers can look you up and buy your details**, and that the US intelligence community's own report warns this kind of data can "facilitate blackmail, stalking, harassment, and public shaming" (ODNI, see Sources below). That's just as alarming, and it's true. Keep the punch and lose the risk. Do not say that scammers or stalkers buy from specific sites: the one government source we had for that (the CFPB's Dec 2024 proposal) was withdrawn in May 2025.

## Calling them what they are (without getting sued)

We're allowed to show contempt. The rule is to aim it at **the industry and the practice**, using **opinion and colourful language**, and never to make **false factual accusations** about a **named company**. Calling an industry "vultures" is rhetoric. Saying "Spokeo steals your data" is a factual claim of a crime, and it's false: they collect legally.

**Safe names for the industry**

| Term | Feel |
|---|---|
| **data dealers** | the headliner; it sounds like drug dealers and is literally accurate |
| **people-sellers** / **the people-selling business** | blunt and true: the product is you |
| **info peddlers** | old-fashioned contempt, good for the traditional audience |
| **privacy profiteers** | they profit off your privacy; reads as opinion |
| **data vultures** | hyperbole; fine for the industry, never next to one company's name |
| **snoop shops** | folksy, sticks in the memory |
| **address auctioneers** | concrete: your address goes to the highest (or any) bidder |
| **data hoarders** | they collect everything about you and keep it |
| **the creep economy** | the whole business of selling who you are |
| **middlemen of your private life** | longer, good for explainer pages |

**Old-time names, for those who get it** (1800s–1900s slang; insults that are opinion, not accusations)

| Term | Where it comes from | Use it like |
|---|---|---|
| **Paul Prys** | Nosy meddler from an 1825 stage comedy; "a regular Paul Pry" | "The internet's Paul Prys have your address on a shelf." |
| **nosy parkers** | Early-1900s slang for a busybody | "Nosy parkers, selling you by the dozen." |
| **rubberneckers** | 1890s American slang for gawkers | "Rubberneckers pay a dollar to look at your front door." |
| **eavesdroppers** | Old English: one who stands under the eaves to listen | "Eavesdroppers built a business on your life." |
| **keyhole peepers** | Old-fashioned snoops | "Keep the keyhole peepers out." |
| **rag-and-bone men** | Victorian scrap collectors who resold whatever they picked up | "The rag-and-bone men of the internet pick up scraps of your life and sell them by the pound." |
| **hucksters / hawkers** | Old words for pushy street sellers | "Hucksters hawking your home address." |
| **gossipmongers / busybodies** | Classic | "The busybody business." |
| **varmints** | Rural American for pests on your property | "Run the varmints off your property." |
| **polecats / buzzards** | Southern and rural contempt | "Shoo the buzzards off." |
| **scoundrels / rascals** | Old-fashioned, theatrical; reads as opinion | "Show the scoundrels the door." |

Slogans in that voice:
- Run the varmints off your property.
- The internet's rag-and-bone men are hauling off your private life. Take it back.
- Don't let Paul Pry peddle your porch.
- Nosy parkers had their day. Shut the gate.
- Shoo the buzzards. Keep your name.

**Old-time words that sound fun but are trouble** (they accuse someone of fraud or a crime, or carry other baggage): *grifters, flimflam men, snake-oil salesmen, mountebanks, swindlers, pickpockets, body snatchers* (all imply fraud or crime); *carpetbaggers* and *scalawags* (Reconstruction-era political baggage, which matters with a Southern audience); *Pinkertons* (a real, trademarked company that still exists); *Peeping Tom* (sexual connotation).

**Words to avoid about them** (they claim a crime or a fact we can't prove)
- "thieves", "steal", "stolen", "illegal", "criminals", "scammers" (they *sell to* scammers; they aren't scammers), "hackers", "terrorists"
- Any insult attached to a **named** broker. On per-site pages, stay factual: "Spokeo lists addresses and phone numbers. Here's how to opt out."

## Never use

- "Erase yourself from the internet", "100% removed", "guaranteed"
- "Cyber terrorists", "criminals" or "hackers" to mean the data brokers themselves
- "They have to answer", "the law makes them" (true only in states with a broker privacy law; say "where your state's law applies")
- Countdown timers, "act now", red alarm graphics
- Any party, candidate, network or politician
- Claims that we stop phone listening, wiretaps or government surveillance
