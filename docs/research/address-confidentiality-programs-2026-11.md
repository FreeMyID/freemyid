# BL-IN-73: Address Confidentiality Programs (Priya Shah, access 2026-11-08)

## What they are
NASS issue briefing, updated January 2026 (page read as PDF), https://www.nass.org/sites/default/files/2025-12/Voting%20and%20State%20Address%20Confidentiality%20Programs%20January%202026%20Update.pdf: ACPs (called Safe at Home in some states) protect people whose safety is at risk (domestic violence, stalking, harassment, rape). Participants get a free, forwardable substitute address or PO box for driver's licences, school enrolment, voter registration and other public records. Enrolment is usually through a trained application assistant or counsellor at a victim-assistance centre; the application is sent to the ACP office for review. Each state differs.
Maryland SOS FAQ (page), https://sos.maryland.gov/ACP/Pages/FAQ.aspx: "Yes, 38 other states have address confidentiality programs" (older count; NASS 2026 is higher).

Plain summary for readers: "If you are fleeing an abuser or stalker, your state may let you use a substitute mailing address instead of your home address on public records. You apply through a trained helper at a victim-services agency, not on your own. This keeps your address off voter lists and many public records. It does not remove you from people-search sites; do those separately."
That last sentence is our inference: none of the pages above say how ACPs interact with people-search sites. Mark as editorial, not sourced. Not read: any page stating what an ACP does about data brokers.
