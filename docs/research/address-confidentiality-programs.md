# Address confidentiality programs (ACP) by state

Backlog item BL-IN-73. Researcher: Priya Shah [freemyid]. Accessed 2026-11-08.
Scope: NC, SC and AZ first (family states), then all 50 states plus DC.

What an ACP is, in plain words: a state gives a person at risk (usually a
survivor of domestic violence, stalking or sexual assault) a substitute mailing
address, often a P.O. box, and forwards their mail. State and local agencies
must accept that address when they create new records, which helps keep the
real home address out of new public records. Private companies and the federal
government generally do not have to accept it.

Limits (check the person's own state program before relying on it):
- It does not remove an address that is already in public records or that a broker
  already has. Illinois: "The ACP and government agencies cannot remove or delete
  information already in public records, including addresses."
- Property records are often not covered. Illinois: the ACP "cannot ensure
  confidentiality for property records". North Carolina: the ACP cannot "keep the
  address of a victim who owns real property (house or land) in her/his name out of
  the public records". Vermont: does not protect land records.
- States allow exceptions for some agencies and records, and the rules differ by state.
- It works best after a move to an address the abuser does not know and that is not
  already tied to the person's name online (Illinois AG page).
So it reduces new public-record exposure; it does not guarantee it. Tell the user to use the program together with the opt-out sweep, not
instead of it, and to talk to an advocate first.

Not legal advice. No real people's data is used here.

## How to read the table

- **Apply:** `Advocate` means the state requires (or its page describes only) an
  in-person trained Application Assistant, usually at a victim-services
  agency. `Self` means the page says a person can apply without one.
  `Not stated` means the page I could read does not say.
- **Voter / DMV:** what the official page says. `Not stated` is not the same as "no".
- Status: `Read` = I read the state's official page on the access date.
  `List only` = the program name and agency come from the lists in the sources
  below, but the state's own page was blocked (HTTP 403/402/404), empty, or not
  fetched, so eligibility, how to apply, voter and DMV coverage are UNVERIFIED.
- "Typical" in the Who-qualifies cell means only the general pattern from NCFFC
  2023 (every state program includes domestic violence, sexual assault and
  stalking). Do not show a "Typical" cell to users as fact.

## Notes for product use

- Family states: NC and SC both route through an Application Assistant at a victim-services agency; neither page describes self-application. The wizard should point to the state page and a hotline and never collect ACP details.
- Several programs say private businesses need not accept the substitute address. Data brokers are private, so an ACP address generally will not stop a broker listing by itself.
- Some programs require voting by mail or absentee ballot to keep the home address private (IL, KY per their pages). Vermont does not: Safe at Home voters can also vote in person as "Voter #XXX Blind Ballot" without saying their address. Mention the state's rule to users who vote in person.
- Arkansas is license-only. California Safe at Home is not the California Delete Act DROP.
- Do not store or log ACP card numbers anywhere in the app.
