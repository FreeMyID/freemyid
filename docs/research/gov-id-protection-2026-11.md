## IRS Identity Protection PIN
Source (page): https://www.irs.gov/identity-theft-fraud-scams/get-an-identity-protection-pin
- Eligibility: anyone with an SSN or ITIN who can verify identity. Parents or legal guardians can request one for a dependent. If you or the dependent are under 18, you must use an alternative to the online methods.
- Ways to get one: (1) IRS app or online account, fastest, in the IP PIN section of your profile (register and verify identity first). (2) Form 15227, if AGI on the last return is under $84,000 (single) or $168,000 (married filing jointly), with an SSN or ITIN and phone access; the IRS calls to verify and the PIN is mailed, usually in 4 to 6 weeks. (3) In person at a Taxpayer Assistance Center by appointment: one federal or state photo ID plus one more ID; for a dependent, your documents plus two IDs for the dependent; PIN usually mailed within 3 weeks.
- Renewal: valid one calendar year; a new one each year. Online enrollees retrieve it themselves, generally shown mid-January to mid-November.
- Protects against: someone else filing a federal return with your SSN or ITIN. You must use it on any federal return you file in the year, including prior-year returns (Forms 1040, 1040-NR, 1040-PR, 1040-SR, 1040-SS). It does NOT protect credit or benefits.
- Cautions: the IRS never asks for the PIN by phone, email or text. Lost PIN: do not file Form 15227 again; use the IRS lost-PIN instructions.
- Matches the existing checklist entry irs_ip_pin; no change needed. Not stated on the page: whether you can drop out (checklist already says so).

## SSA my Social Security account and blocking electronic access
Pages https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount/ and https://www.ssa.gov/faqs/en/questions/KA-01687.html returned 403 today. Facts below are from search summaries of SSA pages (snippet): https://www.ssa.gov/pubs/EN-05-10220.pdf, https://www.ssa.gov/faqs/en/questions/KA-01687.html, https://www.ssa.gov/blog/en/posts/2021-05-20.html.
- Block: call 1-800-772-1213 (TTY 1-800-325-0778) and ask to Block Electronic Access. Then "No one, including you, will be able to see or change your personal information on the internet or through our automated telephone service." To undo, call and prove identity. One publication gives hours as 8 a.m. to 7 p.m. weekdays.
- Why claim an account: SSA says the account protects against someone else creating one for you; the exact "thief redirects benefits" wording is in older leaflets, not read directly. The block does not stop a person at an SSA office from changing records after proving identity (not stated; do not claim).
- Status: unverified for public use until a person reads ssa.gov in a browser. Existing checklist entry ssa_my_social_security (verification: partial) is correct as is.

## USPS Informed Delivery
Source (page): https://www.usps.com/manage/informed-delivery.htm
- Eligibility: residential, business or PO Box address in an eligible ZIP; a "uniquely coded" mailbox is needed; business street or PO Box addresses need a USPS.com business account; some apartments or condos may not qualify.
- Sign-up: opt in through a USPS.com account, or use the sign-up link, check the address, give account info and verify identity. If online verification fails, go to a USPS location offering Identity Verification Services. Notifications typically start within 3 business days.
- Shows: grayscale images of the address side of letter-sized mail processed on automated equipment (colour for some items like catalogs), plus package tracking.
- What it protects against: the USPS page does NOT say it prevents mail theft or stops someone else signing up for your address. Keep the existing note: do not publish "claiming your address stops someone else enrolling" as USPS's words. USPS recommends adding multifactor authentication to the account.
- Existing checklist entry usps_informed_delivery is consistent (it says "check your mailbox is eligible" and "3 business days" can be added).
