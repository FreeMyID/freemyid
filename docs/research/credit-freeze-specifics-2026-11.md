# BL-IN-71: credit freeze specifics (Priya Shah, access date 2026-11-08 unless noted)

"Page" = fetched and read. "Snippet" = only a search-result summary (page returned 403). Only page-level facts should go in public copy.

## 1. Rules that apply to all three big bureaus (federal law)
Source (page): 15 U.S.C. 1681c-1, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c-1 (first 100,000 of 118,245 characters read; sub-section (j)(4) removal rules not read).
- Placing a freeze: within 1 business day of a toll-free phone or secure electronic request, within 3 business days of a mailed request ((i)(2)(A)).
- Removing or lifting: within 1 hour of a phone or electronic request, within 3 business days for mail ((i)(3)(C)). Same time frames for a temporary lift (CFPB page below).
- Existing accounts: the freeze does not apply to a company you already have an account with, its affiliates, or collection agencies acting for it, when they review or collect that account ((i)(4)(A), (i)(5)).
- Free: FTC, https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-credit-freezes-fraud-alerts (page): "There's no cost to place or lift a credit freeze." Lasts until you lift it. Anyone can place one, no ID theft needed. FTC suggests lifting only at the bureau the lender will check. The FTC page does NOT give speed, channels or phone numbers.
- CFPB, https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-credit-freeze-or-security-freeze-on-my-credit-report-en-1341/ (page): a freeze "prevents prospective creditors from accessing your credit file"; it also stops you opening new credit. Still able to see your file: creditors on accounts you already hold, certain government entities such as child support agencies, and companies you hired to monitor your file. You must contact each of Equifax, Experian and TransUnion separately.

## 3. Protected-consumer freeze (someone who cannot act: child under 16, or an incapacitated adult with a guardian/conservator)
- Who qualifies: 15 U.S.C. 1681c-1(j)(1)(B): a person under 16 when the request is made, or an incapacitated person or protected person with an appointed guardian or conservator. Representative = anyone giving the agency "sufficient proof of authority" ((j)(1)(C)). Page: Cornell link above. FTC explains it for financial caregivers, https://consumer.ftc.gov/managing-someone-elses-money-new-protection-id-theft-fraud (page).
- Proof of authority (statute (j)(1)(F), FTC): a court order, or a valid power of attorney; for a child, a government document showing parentage (statute also covers foster-care certification).
- Proof of identity of the representative (statute (j)(1)(G), FTC): Social Security card or number, certified birth certificate, or government-issued ID such as a driver's license.
- Free ((j)(2)(A)); placed within 1 business day of phone/electronic request or 3 business days of mail; confirmation within 5 business days ((j)(2)(B)). Freeze applies at all three bureaus, each one separately.
- Equifax, incapacitated adult, official form (read as an image today), https://assets.equifax.com/assets/personal/Incapacitated_Adult_Freeze_Request_Form.pdf: mail only. Guardian provides one item from each category: (identity) a copy of a driver's license or other government ID, a copy of the Social Security card, a copy of the birth certificate; (authority) a court order or a lawfully executed, valid power of attorney. The incapacitated adult: copy of Social Security card AND copy of birth certificate. Form also covers temporary lift and permanent removal (free). Mail to Equifax Security Freeze, P.O. Box 105788, Atlanta, GA 30348. Equifax minors (under 16): parent, guardian or POA via its Minor Freeze Request form (page).
- Experian minors: parents/guardians can freeze the file of a child under 16; Experian says minor requests must be by mail. The page says nothing about incapacitated adults, so I did not find Experian's own protected-consumer page.
- TransUnion: snippet only (see above). The three bureaus do not share one request.
- Conflict to note for the article: the Equifax page (read today) says minor and incapacitated forms are separate Equifax forms, and that protected-consumer freezes must be done at Experian and TransUnion directly too.

## 4. Fraud alerts (not a freeze)
FTC page: initial alert 1 year, renewable, one bureau must tell the other two; extended alert 7 years needs an FTC identity theft report or police report; active-duty alert 1 year. Free.

## 5. Suggested article wording (public-safe)
"A credit freeze is free. It stops lenders seeing your credit file when someone applies for new credit, so a thief cannot open an account in your name. It does not close your existing accounts, and companies you already do business with can still check your file. You freeze each bureau separately. For a child under 16, or an adult with a court-appointed guardian or conservator, the guardian or someone holding a valid power of attorney can place the freeze by sending proof of who they are and proof of their authority, such as a court order or power of attorney."
