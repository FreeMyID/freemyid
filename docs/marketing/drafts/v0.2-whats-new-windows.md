## Page version

# What's new in Free My ID 0.2

**Short version:** if you already have Free My ID, download the new installer from freemyid.org and run it, the same way you did before. It updates the one you have. You do not need to uninstall first or set anything up again.

If you are new, welcome. Free My ID asks the people-search and data-broker sites that list your name, address and phone number to take your details down, and keeps checking. It runs on your own computer. There is no account, and nothing is sent to us.

## What you will notice

**A "Today" screen.** It shows what needs you now, and nothing else. There is also a page for each site Free My ID knows about.

**"Where am I listed?"** Before anything is sent, Free My ID can look up your name on the people-search sites it is able to read. It gives you a score from 0 to 100, with the reasons in plain words. The score always tells you how many sites it could actually check. A low score does not mean you are safe. It only speaks for the sites that were checked. Some sites block automated visits. Free My ID does not try to get around that. It gives you a link so you can look yourself.

**"Is this you?"** If a site shows a possible match, Free My ID asks you. Nothing is done about a listing until you say it is yours.

**Protect yourself checklists.** Free step-by-step guides, such as freezing your credit. You tick each step off as you go. Your ticks are stored, encrypted, on your computer. The same guides are on freemyid.org/protect/ if you want to print them. (Check that the page is live before publishing.)

**Older addresses (your choice).** You can turn on one extra search under a previous address. It is off unless you turn it on.

**California.** If you live in California, the app points you to the state's free DROP request first. (DROP is the state's own tool: one request that goes to the data brokers registered in California.)

**A printable summary** of what was found and what was done.

## Smaller things that are better

- Uninstalling is smoother. If Free My ID is still open, the uninstaller closes it for you.
- The email-setup screen for Proton Mail users uses calmer, plainer words.
- The password screen hint now says what it really does.
- The final "Ready" screen stays on purpose, so you can see it worked.
- Your saved details are stored encrypted on your computer. This version handles the key to that store more carefully.

## What has not changed

- No account, no cloud, no tracking.
- Your details are stored only on your computer. They go out only in a removal request you approve, straight to the site that request is for.
- Free My ID never asks for your Social Security number, bank details or a card.
- Nothing real is sent until you say so.

## Please know

- This is the first release of the "see where you are listed" part. It reads a limited set of sites, and it is a first look, not a full check. More comes in 0.3.
- Free My ID works on people-search and data-broker sites. It does not change what Facebook or Meta does with your account, and it does not stop hacking, wiretaps or phone "listening".
- It asks sites to remove you. In some states the law says they must answer. In many places they do not have to, and some will not reply. Sites also list people again, which is why Free My ID keeps checking.
- Windows may show a blue box that says "Windows protected your PC". That is because we have not bought a publisher certificate yet, not because anything is wrong. The download page shows exactly what to click, and how to check the file with the checksum.
- The first run uses the free sample list of sites. The larger list is a separate, later step.
- The installer and the scheduled task have not been re-checked on a fresh Windows 10 or 11 machine for this release (RELEASE_v0.2 "NOT tested"). Say this honestly if asked; do not drop it.
