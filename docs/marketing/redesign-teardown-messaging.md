# Website redesign: competitor teardown, messaging and information architecture

Author: brand-marketing, 2026-11-07. Draft only. Nothing here is built or published. Competitor facts below were read from each home page on 2026-11-07 (one fetch each; pricing and how-it-works sub-pages were not separately fetched, so pricing details are only what the home pages showed). Competitor names appear in this internal doc only, never on the site.

Why the current site feels amateur (from `site/index.html` and `docs/WEBSITE.md`): the heading order is "promises, the post, 3 steps, does/doesn't, helping a parent, FAQ", so the visitor gets a lot of reading before any proof. There is no visual product proof above the fold beyond a drawn mockup, the "civic" serif plus flag motif reads as a hobby or political site rather than software, and the nav is thin ("The app / Help"). The content is honest, but it is not organised the way trusted software sites are.

### Cross-site patterns
- Hero = outcome headline + one sentence + one primary button above the fold. Secondary button is almost always "for business" or "see how".
- A second trust element sits directly under the hero (logo strip or a callout). Always before the process.
- Process is always 3 to 6 numbered steps, usually followed by a dashboard or report picture.
- Third-party proof dominates: press logos, awards, SOC 2 / ISO badges, review stars, counters, money-back guarantees.
- Pricing is either a plan table or a single price. All use "from $x" or a discount to anchor.
- Dark patterns we will NOT use: promo banners, discount modals, chat widgets, "free scan" email capture, fear stats, "#1" claims, unlabelled "0+" counters.
- Common FAQ topics: what is a data broker, how long does it take, will I be removed from everything, is it safe to give you my info, can I cancel, what do you do with my data, how do you differ from doing it myself.

6. Add a no-names comparison table ("Free My ID vs. paid removal services vs. by hand") that includes an honest "where paid services are better" row. Source or soften every category claim.

8. Simplify the nav to How it works / What it removes / Is it safe? / For families / Guides / FAQ plus a sticky Download button. Drop sign-in items and say in the footer there is no account.
9. Use build-derived real numbers only (guide count, registry sample count, release version/size). No hard-coded counters, no "trusted by", no ratings. Hold the "hundreds of brokers" line until broker-researcher confirms the count.
10. Do not show a Download button that goes nowhere. `release.yaml` is currently null, so decide before launch: fall back to "coming soon" with a GitHub link, or hold the redesign until a release exists. Also confirm the "15 minutes", "six screens" and "daily" claims on a real install.
