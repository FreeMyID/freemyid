# How to remove yourself from people-search sites, for free

If you've searched your own name and found your address, phone number or relatives' names on a website, you're not alone, and you can do something about it. Many of these sites have a free way to ask them to take your listing down [S1]. Here is how to do it yourself. You don't need to buy anything, and you don't need our app.

## What these sites are

People-search sites (also called data brokers) collect public records and other information and put together a profile of a person. Then they sell access to it [S1]. Your name, address and phone number may be on a dozen of them at once, often copied from one to another.

## Before you start

- **Pick an email address just for removals.** Most sites email you a link to confirm the request [S1]. A separate address keeps those emails, and any marketing that follows, out of your main inbox. (This is our advice, not a rule.)
- **Write down how your listing looks.** Sites usually ask for your name and the city or address exactly as they show it [S1].
- **Never pay to opt out, and never give a Social Security number or bank details.** The guides we publish don't need either [S1]. If a "removal" page asks for card details, stop.

## Step by step

1. **Search for yourself** on each site, using the name and the city you're listed under.
2. **Open that site's own opt-out page.** Use the link in our guide for that site. Look-alike pages exist, so check you're on the site's own address [S1].
3. **Find your listing and ask for removal.** Most sites ask you to pick your listing, then enter an email address [S1].
4. **Click the link in the confirmation email.** On many sites the request doesn't count until you do [S1].
5. **Write down the date.** Some sites take days, others weeks [S1].
6. **Check again in a few months.** These sites can list people again when they buy new records [S2].

Our free step-by-step guides cover each site one at a time, including Whitepages, Spokeo, TruePeopleSearch, BeenVerified, FastPeopleSearch, Intelius and MyLife: **[See all opt-out guides](/guides/)** [S1].

## If you live in California

California runs a free state website called DROP. One request there reaches more than 600 registered data brokers. It's only for California residents, and you sign in with the State of California's own sign-in [S3].

## Other states

Some states have privacy laws that require a company to answer a deletion request. In other states, a removal request is still worth sending: many sites honor it, but they don't have to [S4]. Our guides say which approach each site uses.

## Search results that still show up

Removing the listing from the site is the step that matters. After that, Google may still show the old page for a while. It drops the page on its own schedule, and Google's "Refresh outdated content" tool can speed this up [S5]. Google also has a "Results about you" tool that can hide search results showing your phone number, home address or email. It hides the results but doesn't delete the information from the sites themselves [S6].

## Doing it for a parent or grandparent

You can do all of this on someone else's behalf, sitting with them. Use their details, and let them see each step. Our [guide for families](/families/) covers this, with a printable card.

## If you'd rather not do it by hand

Free My ID is a free, open-source Windows app that does these steps for you. Your details are kept on your computer and go only to the sites you ask to remove you. Nothing is sent to us [S7]. It re-checks later, because sites can list people again [S2]. It can't promise every site will comply, and nobody honest can. [Download Free My ID](/download/)
