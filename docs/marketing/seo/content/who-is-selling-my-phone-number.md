# Get your phone number off people-search sites

Typing a phone number into a people-search site often brings up a name and address. That is what these sites sell: profiles built from public records and other data, linked by details like your number. [S1]

## Why your number is there

Phone numbers reach these sites from public records, marketing lists and other data sources. We do not claim to know which source put your number on a given site. [S1]

## What you can do

1. Search your own number and name, and note each site that lists you. [S1]
2. Use each site's own removal page. Our step-by-step guides: https://freemyid.org/guides/spokeo/, https://freemyid.org/guides/whitepages/, https://freemyid.org/guides/truepeoplesearch/ and more at https://freemyid.org/guides/. [S1]
3. Remove the listing, not just the number: sites usually remove the whole profile when you ask. [S2]
4. Use a removal-only email address, and never give a card or a Social Security number. [S1]
5. Check again in a few months, since sites can list people again. [S3]

## What this will not fix

Removing listings does not stop spam calls on its own, and it does not touch your carrier or phone. To report unwanted calls, use the FTC's Do Not Call registry (needs-verify wording and URL: broker-researcher). [S4]

## Doing it automatically

Free My ID is a free, open-source Windows app that sends the removal requests for you and re-checks on a schedule. No account. https://freemyid.org/
