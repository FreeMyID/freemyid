Does the "I do not consent" post work?

{# source: docs/USER_GUIDE.md section 11 "I posted I do not consent" (approved); POSITIONING.md rule 1 #}

Short answer: no, but your instinct was right. Here's why the post can't do the job, and what can.

{{ icon('check') }}

You were right to object. Your name, address and phone number are your information. Wanting a say in who sells them is reasonable.

The post you may have seen

Lots of people have posted something like "I do not give permission to use my pictures or information…" on Facebook. It's often shared by caring people who want to protect their friends.

Why it can't reach the people selling your details

{# source: USER_GUIDE section 11 (post does not reach the sites); faq.yaml "comes-back" (sites use public records and buy data from one another); PLAN_V1 D1 (each site has its own removal channel, from the registry) #}
  • {{ icon('dash') }}A post on your page goes to your friends' feeds. The companies that sell your details don't see it, and they aren't required to read it.
  • {{ icon('dash') }}Those companies are mostly people-search sites (also called data brokers). Many of them list people who've never had a Facebook account at all, using public records and purchased data.
  • {{ icon('dash') }}Each site has its own way of taking removal requests. A request only counts when it goes to that site, in the form it processes.

What actually works

{# source: step 1 = /guides/ (registry); step 2 = POSITIONING.md "Legal accuracy", brokers/state_laws.yaml; step 3 = registry recheck_interval_days (90 to 180 days); step 4 = USER_GUIDE section 11 "Does this change what Facebook does?" #}
  1. 1

    Send your "I do not consent" to each site

    Ask each people-search site to remove your listing, through its own removal process. Our free opt-out guides show you how for some of the best-known sites.

  2. 2

    Use the law where you have it

    California and a growing number of states have privacy laws that require these sites to answer removal requests. In other states the request is a polite one. Many sites honor it anyway, but some don't.

  3. 3

    Keep checking

    Sites buy fresh data and can list people again. Check back every few months.

  4. 4

    For Facebook itself, use Facebook's own settings

    Removing yourself from people-search sites doesn't change what Facebook does with your account. That's done in the privacy settings inside Facebook.
