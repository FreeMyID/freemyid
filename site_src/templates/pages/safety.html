Is Free My ID safe?

{# source: old privacy page lead (approved copy); teardown section 8 H2; second sentence per WR-28 (not absolute) #}

A privacy tool you have to trust with your information isn't much of a privacy tool. Free My ID is built so you can check our work.

Your details stay on your computer. Nothing is sent to us.

{# source: teardown section 8 column 1; D8 "Nothing is sent to us" (src/freemyid/netpolicy.py, shared rule 4) #}

Your details stay on your PC. The app talks only to the sites you are asking to remove you, your own email account, and our public list of those sites. Nothing is sent to us.

    {# source: old privacy page "Where your information lives" (approved copy) #}
  • {{ icon('computer') }}Only on your computer. There's no account and no cloud. We have no servers holding your data.
    • {# source: D8, src/freemyid/crypto.py (AES-256-GCM), storage/db.py; key in the Windows credential store #}
  • {{ icon('lock') }}Locked. Your details are stored encrypted on your computer, and the key is kept in Windows' own secure password store. The printable progress report you can make is a normal file, so it holds only the name you gave this person, site names, statuses and dates, never your address or phone number.{# source: SEC-43 ruling; wizard/report.py docstring (PII-light: label, site names, states, dates) #}
    • {# source: src/freemyid/netpolicy.py; old privacy page "Fenced in" (approved copy) #}
  • {{ icon('shield') }}Fenced in. The app may only contact the removal sites themselves and your removal mailbox. Everything else is blocked. The only exception is a one-time, optional download of a small extra browser, and only if your computer has no Chrome or Edge, and it asks you first.
    • {# source: shared rule 6 (Listing.shown_fields); old privacy page (approved copy) #}
  • {{ icon('check') }}Sites get as little as possible: only what they already show about you, plus a removal email address.

Keeping the list of sites up to date

{# source: old privacy page "Keeping the list of sites up to date" (approved copy); configured registry URL, shared rule 4 #}

People-search sites change their removal pages often. So Free My ID can download an updated list of sites and removal steps from the Free My ID project. That download sends nothing about you or the people you protect: it only fetches the list.

What it never asks for

    {# source: old privacy page "We will never ask for" (approved copy); POSITIONING.md rule 4 #}
  • {{ icon('x') }}Your Social Security number
  • {{ icon('x') }}Bank account, credit or debit card details
    • {# source: teardown section 8 column 2; shared rule 6 ("Never pay") #}
  • {{ icon('x') }}A payment of any kind
  • {{ icon('x') }}Your full birthday. The setup asks only for a year of birth, and you can leave that blank
    • {# source: teardown section 8 column 2; shared rule 6 (phone only with the Profile.permissions flag) #}
  • {{ icon('x') }}Your phone number, unless you choose to share it with a site that needs it
  • {{ icon('x') }}A photo of your ID, unless you switch that on yourself in Settings for a site that insists on it
    • {# WR-30: only the everyday email password in the list; app password explained below #}
  • {{ icon('x') }}The password to your everyday email
{# source: families.html Gmail step (separate free mailbox + app password); PLAN_V1 D2 (mailbox optional) #}

If you want Free My ID to send requests for you, you give it an app password for a separate removal mailbox. That's optional.

{{ icon('shield') }}

If anything claiming to be Free My ID asks you for these, it isn't us.

Windows may warn "unknown publisher" the first time, because the installer is not yet signed. See exactly what the warning looks like and what to click.

Stop or uninstall any time

{# source: download page "Removing it" (approved copy); packaging/windows/freemyid.iss [UninstallRun] "schedule remove" removes the daily task #}

Open Windows Settings → Apps, find Free My ID, and click Uninstall. That also removes the daily check. It asks whether to delete your saved records too.

What it will not do

{# source: teardown section 8 closing line; POSITIONING.md "Lines we do NOT cross" #}

Free My ID does not stop phone listening, wiretaps or government surveillance. It removes listings from people-search sites.

This website

{# source: old privacy page "This website" (approved copy); CSP in docs/WEBSITE.md #}

No cookies, no trackers, no analytics, no outside fonts or scripts. Every picture, icon and style on this site comes from this site itself, so visiting it doesn't tell any other company that you were here.

That's also why you didn't see a cookie banner: there are no cookies to ask about. The site has no forms, so it can't collect anything you type.

Links to other websites (such as a people-search site's removal page, or our code on GitHub) are ordinary links. Once you click one, that website's own privacy rules apply.
